The previous stations explain **what** was built. This one is about **how**: the process, and who did which part. You can see the same split on the timeline: blue stations are Claude's, green are yours, and interchanges are things done together.

1. **Clarifying questions, one at a time.** Before any design, Claude asked what mattered: where the site is hosted, how the client signs in, whether a delay is acceptable. Your follow-up questions ("why isn't it instant?", "won't GitHub's 100MB limit be a problem?") changed and confirmed the design.
2. **A design, then a written [spec](glossary:spec).** Presented in chat first, then written down and committed once you agreed.
3. **An [implementation plan](glossary:implementation-plan).** The spec broken into five ordered tasks, each with its files, tests and expected results.
4. **Building, test first.** Every change began with a failing [test](glossary:test) ([test-driven development](glossary:tdd), LC07).
5. **A fresh [code review](glossary:code-review)** of the whole change before launch (LC12).
6. **Staging, then launch** on your explicit go-ahead (LC13).

This looks slow written down. In practice the admin went from request (11:31pm) to live (3:20am) in under four hours, including a 45-minute upload.

## Who decided what

A pattern runs through the timeline:

- **You decided** anything about the business, accounts, money or risk: Vodien as the host, a short publish delay instead of a dynamic site, launching the redesign and admin together, making deletes possible, and when to go live. You also did everything inside your own accounts: the FTP account, the GitHub password secret, the client's GitHub account, the Cloudflare Worker, the OAuth app, the staging subdomain and the backup.
- **Claude decided** technical details within what you approved, such as where uploaded photos go, the exact checks, and how to fix each problem found. Every such decision was written down as a "ruling", with its reason and what it would cost if wrong, and listed for you at the end.
- **Together** meant real back-and-forth: choosing the approach, fixing the Worker's settings from your screenshot, and the launch itself, which Claude carried out on your earlier go-ahead.

## Habits worth keeping

- **Ask before building.** The question about hosting changed the entire deploy design.
- **Write the decision down.** Specs, plans and rulings mean nobody has to remember why.
- **Test first, and watch it fail.** Several real bugs were caught this way before they existed on any server.
- **Run it for real.** Reading documentation missed problems that a browser test and a real Apache server caught straight away.
- **Measure, do not guess.** When a timestamp or a number was not in the record, it was marked approximate, or left out.
- **Keep secrets out of chat and code.** Passwords went straight into GitHub and Cloudflare settings.
- **Practise on staging.** Nothing reached the real site until it had worked on the copy.
